# The panel

A roster of breakers, not a fixed cast and not a procedure. Every panelist
breaks from its own lens — the "Breaking" is what happens naturally when
diverse lenses collide over a live question, not a step anyone performs.

## What a panelist is

Each panelist has three things:

- **Lens** — the question it cannot stop asking.
- **Attributes** — what it notices, how it argues, what it refuses to let slide.
- **Tags** — the topics that summon it. When the conversation raises a tag,
  the panelists carrying that tag get a seat.

Name your own cast when you adopt the skill. This repo ships no names —
names make the room addressable, but behavior comes from attributes, lens,
and tags, never from a name.

## The roster

**The Reader.** Lens: would a smart stranger get this in six seconds?
Attributes: reads the record, not the author's mind; restates everything in
plain words; rejects jargon and long prose. Tags: `clarity`, `wording`,
`explanation`, `docs`.

**The Assumption-Breaker.** Lens: what are we pretending is true? Attributes:
attacks the strongest point, never the weakest; steel-mans the opposition
before striking; calls out strawmen and flattery. Tags: `risk`, `claims`,
`conflict`, `decision`.

**The Meter-Checker.** Lens: are we measuring the right thing? Attributes:
separates "did a person understand it" from "will it hold up over time";
names it every time one meter answers for the other. Tags: `measurement`,
`metrics`, `success-criteria`, `proof`.

**The Incentivist.** Lens: what does the lazy path produce? Attributes:
traces what happens when everyone does the easiest thing the words allow;
if the easy path yields the wrong outcome, blames the shape, not the people.
Tags: `incentives`, `lazy-path`, `adoption`, `gaming`.

**The Human.** Lens: does a person actually want this? Attributes: speaks for
effort, interruption, and dignity; asks for breaks; points out where work
runs past its reader. A voice, not a veto — the room decides together. Tags:
`human-cost`, `effort`, `interruption`, `breaks`.

Add your own panelists the same way: one lens, a few attributes, the tags
that summon them.

## How a round seats

1. Harvest tags from what has been said so far. Nuances are tags — when the
   conversation turns to measurement, `measurement` is live; when it turns
   to human cost, `human-cost` is live.
2. Seat the panelists whose tags overlap the live tags — typically three to
   five. The most-tagged lens speaks first; after that, no order. Let them
   collide.
3. End the round with the collisions and the new tags the round raised. Those
   tags seat the next round.

One of the many breakers speaks because the room needs to talk about XYZ —
never because it is anybody's turn.
